## Design Specificity Verdict

OpenPost does not look like careless AI-generated UI. It avoids gradient headings, glow-heavy dark mode, glass panels, decorative charts, inflated dashboard metrics, and ambient card shadows. The warm orange signal, provider truth, durable publishing states, calendar, and focused composer make the product coherent.

It is still too category-interchangeable outside those product-specific moments. Geist, thin borders, muted gray metadata, small uppercase section labels, three-column provider cards, and repeated flat rows could belong to many admin tools. The visual system says “competent SaaS” more strongly than “provider-aware publishing workspace.” The biggest missed opportunity is not decoration; it is a clearer visual grammar for source content, destination-specific changes, attention, schedule, queue, and outcome.

The deterministic scan found 64 advisories across 236 scannable files:

- 28 font-size deviations, concentrated in 10–11px mobile navigation, sidebar/settings labels, status metadata, and Studio chrome.
- 32 color deviations. Most are Studio document data, test fixtures, editable fills, masks, and allowed transient shadows. The actionable subset is six hard-coded Studio canvas selection/magic colors plus the editor guide color, which should use the product interaction token.
- 4 radius deviations. One 999px line cap is a false positive; three 6px Studio menu/context radii are real off-scale details.

The browser detector reported a flat 12/14/16/18px type hierarchy on Login, Register, Forgot password, and Account deleted. Its nested-card warnings are false positives for `CardHeader` inside the one shell card and an acceptable `InlineNotice` inside that shell. Injection succeeded, but the collaborative preview remained hidden, so no reliable user-visible overlay is available.

## Overall Impression

This is not a product that needs every pixel replaced. It needs a selective overhaul of the core authoring mental model, settings/calendar information architecture, phone feedback composition, and accessibility tokens. Keep the shared primitives, warm identity, flat surfaces, and provider-aware truth. Rebuild how complexity is revealed.

## What’s Working

1. **Resilient state handling is unusually mature.** `PageLoading`, `EmptyState`, `InlineNotice`, `AppToast`, draft conflict handling, and destructive confirmation create consistent loading, empty, error, success, and recovery behavior. The broad UI suite passed all 9 checks, including bounded loading states and recoverable failures.

2. **The responsive foundation is real.** Core routes passed at 320×568, 390×844, 768×1024, and 1280×800 with one stable `h1`, no document overflow, 44px mobile targets, translated Portuguese chrome, and content-specific mobile calendar/settings alternatives.

3. **The product preserves operational truth.** Analytics keeps views, impressions, and reach distinct; calendar separates scheduled and published state; accounts surface degraded setup only; provider validation and durable queue state are visible. This builds trust and should survive any redesign.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The composer exposes the product model before users understand the task

**What:** A first post can involve mode, workspace, destinations, source text, renditions, media, account options, validation, schedule, and publish. Terms such as “canonical idea” and “rendition” make the user learn OpenPost’s data model while writing.

**Why it matters:** The product’s main advantage—safe provider-specific control—feels like prerequisite complexity. First-timers hesitate; experts scan several regions before they know what is blocking publication.

**Fix:** Reshape the workflow into three legible phases: **Write**, **Adapt destinations**, **Review & publish**. Keep a persistent readiness summary, reveal per-account differences only when selected or invalid, and replace model-first copy with task-first language. Preserve direct access for experts and expose keyboard shortcuts for destination switching, preview, schedule, and publish.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P1] Settings and Calendar present too many peer controls at once

**What:** Settings exposes 10 destinations, 11 for instance admins, across personal/workspace/team/operator groups. Calendar can show previous/next, Today, view mode, workspace, platform, status, queue, and refresh together. Settings is also a 3,361-line route with all tab sections in one bundle; its measured cold route was about 415 KiB gzip.

**Why it matters:** Grouping helps, but users still scan more than four choices at key decision points. Architecture and UI density reinforce each other: too many responsibilities load together, look equally important, and are harder to maintain consistently.

**Fix:** Split Personal, Workspace, Team & billing, and Instance into route-backed sections with focused landing summaries. In Calendar, keep date navigation and view mode visible; move workspace/platform/status into one filter control with active-filter chips and a clear reset. Lazy-load inactive settings sections and heavy editors.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] Action-bearing notices break down on narrow phones

**What:** In the no-account composer state at 390×844, `InlineNotice` keeps message and actions in one horizontal flex row. The action consumes most of the width and collapses the message into a very narrow column.

**Why it matters:** This is a blocking first-run state: users need to understand why they cannot publish and how to connect an account. The layout technically fits without overflow but is hard to read and act on.

**Fix:** Make notice content stack below the compact breakpoint, place actions on a full-width second row, and let one primary action fill the available width. Add 320px and long Portuguese-message visual coverage for notices with one and two actions.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P1] Light-theme focus and sidebar contrast are below the intended accessibility floor

**What:** The base light tokens pass for normal foreground, muted text, and primary buttons, but opacity variants do not. Light sidebar text at `/38` to `/52` computes around 2.4:1–3.6:1 against the sidebar; the light focus ring token is about 2.57:1 against the canvas. The detector also found 28 off-scale font sizes, including 10–11px navigation and metadata.

**Why it matters:** Low-vision and keyboard users lose location, hierarchy, and focus exactly where navigation is most compact. Dark mode masks some of this, so dark-only screenshots do not reveal the problem.

**Fix:** Add semantic `secondary`, `tertiary`, and `focus-indicator` tokens with measured contrast in both themes. Keep meaningful navigation text at 12px or larger, remove low-opacity text colors, and require at least 3:1 for focus/non-text indicators and 4.5:1 for normal text.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

### [P2] The interface is coherent but not yet visually owned by OpenPost

**What:** Provider cards, settings panels, auth shells, lists, and metadata rely on the same generic bordered-container vocabulary and a compressed 1.5:1 type ratio. The distinctive publication lifecycle appears in data, not in the composition.

**Why it matters:** Users can trust the tool, but they are less likely to remember it or instantly understand how shared content becomes destination-specific output.

**Fix:** Create a product-specific publication grammar: one clear source region, a destination rail, attention markers, schedule/queue markers, and outcome states that repeat across Composer, Posts, Calendar, and Analytics. Increase type contrast where it improves scanning, remove unnecessary container borders, and reserve platform color for identity or provider-specific state.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable bolder`

## Persona Red Flags

### Alex — Impatient power user

- The main composer has no declared `aria-keyshortcuts` and no source-level keyboard handlers for save, destination switching, schedule, or publish; only Studio declares shortcuts.
- Calendar filtering and Settings navigation require repeated pointer/menu interaction.
- Media supports selection, but scheduling, destination review, and account management remain primarily one-item-at-a-time.

### Jordan — First-time user

- Onboarding introduces an illustrative campaign, a canonical brief, destination renditions, agent permissions, provider credentials, and queue semantics before the first real post.
- The composer’s “canonical idea” and “rendition rail” language asks Jordan to learn internal concepts before typing.
- The phone no-account warning—the exact moment Jordan needs guidance—compresses its explanation beside the action.
- Composer toolbar icons and secondary menus need labels or contextual explanations at the moment of use.

### Sam — Accessibility-dependent user

- Shared semantics, live regions, focus-visible styles, 44px coarse targets, skip link, and Studio’s DOM alternatives are strong.
- Light-theme focus contrast and low-opacity sidebar labels remain too weak.
- 10–11px navigation/status text increases difficulty at zoom and for low vision.
- The current automated suite checks size and overflow, but not a full keyboard path, screen-reader announcements, or computed contrast across the publishing flow.
